Real-Time Bioacoustic Monitoring

The critically endangered North Atlantic right whale migrates seasonally along the U.S. East Coast to its winter calving grounds in the Southeast U.S. In the shallow waters off South Carolina, Georgia, and Florida, right whales nurse, rest, and socialize near the surface, exposing mothers and calves to a high risk of vessel strike. In northern foraging grounds, real-time passive acoustic monitoring using gliders and buoys is already being used to inform monitoring efforts and communicate detections with vessel captains and managers to help reduce vessel-strike risk.

In the Southeast U.S., glider-based passive acoustic monitoring may also be a useful tool for providing 24/7 coverage, but the shallow-water environment creates uncertainty in how detectable right whales are in this region. To help inform the deployment of real-time instruments in the Southeast U.S., I analyze acoustic dataPassword: listen from three different platform types using LFDCS, Raven, and noise analysis methods. These analyses help evaluate the potential for an acoustic real-time monitoring program that could complement existing aerial monitoring efforts and support NOAA Slow Zone notices, which provide real-time information to mariners about right whale presence and request reduced vessel speeds to less than 10 knots.

Paleoceanographic Proxy Modeling

View Manuscript

The Western North Atlantic has recently undergone rapid ocean warming, affecting key marine species. To investigate whether this warming is recent or part of a longer-term trend, paleoclimate proxies like Arctica islandica ocean quahog shells are used to look beyond modern instrumentation through shell-derived oxygen isotope records.

Isotopic records from six sites, ranging from the Delmarva Peninsula to the northern Gulf of Maine, show large regional variability. To investigate these differences, climate model output and ocean reanalysis data are used to generate simulated clam oxygen isotope records, which are compared to measured shell records from the 1850s to the present. Using proxy system modeling, a better understanding of the specific environmental conditions that influence the oxygen isotope records from each site is gained, enabling a more robust reconstruction of past environmental changes along the western North Atlantic shelf.

Active Acoustic ID of Arctic Fauna

As part of a research project in the Barents Sea, Norwegian Arctic, fish and zooplankton distributions are being studied across the polar front. At dozens of stations, a CTD-Rosette was lowered through the water column with a Wideband Acoustic Transceiver and optical sensors for smaller zooplankton and particles.

The goal is to identify and size fish and zooplankton from WBAT broadband acoustic profiles collected at 38 kHz and 333 kHz. For fish, this work uses resonance peaks and broadband acoustic responses to estimate size. For zooplankton, acoustic size estimates are compared with size spectra from LISST, LOPC, and UVP instruments. These comparisons are used to develop unsupervised classification methods for profiles where UVP data are not available. A broader goal is to help add these methods to open-source software such as Echopype, and develop unsupervised classification methods for sizing.

Water Supply and Bill Payment Typologies

In commercial hubs of countries like Kenya, Ghana, and Uganda, investigating the relationship between intermittent water supply and bill payment can provide insight into best practices for utility companies. Using Nairobi water utility records from 2012-2023, customer billing and payment behavior is modeled to examine how different customers pay bills and accumulate arrears over time.

Gaussian mixture models are used to classify customers into interpretable payment typologies, while XGBoost regression trees and feature engineering help explore the factors associated with payment behavior. These analyses identify high-arrears and low-payment customer groups, offering practical information that can help utilities better understand revenue challenges and customer payment patterns.
